Project Overview

The Dual-Motor Planar Linkage Mechanism is a custom mechanical prototype consisting of interconnected links, pivot joints, and two motor-driven base positions. It was developed to study planar linkage movement and stepper motor actuation. The prototype uses an ESP32 microcontroller, two DM542 stepper motor drivers, two NEMA 23 stepper motors, and a custom mechanical linkage. The ESP32-based system controls the two stepper motors, and their coordinated movement drives the planar linkage according to the coordinates provided to the control system.

Objectives

  • Design and fabricate a planar mechanical linkage.
  • Integrate two stepper motors with independent DM542 drivers.
  • Generate STEP and DIR signals using an ESP32.
  • Drive the planar mechanical linkage through coordinated dual-motor movement according to supplied coordinates.
  • Study the relationship between motor movement and linkage position.
  • Develop a platform for future coordinated motion control.

Components Used

  • ESP32 development board
  • 2 × DM542 stepper motor drivers
  • 2 × NEMA 23 stepper motors
  • 12 V, 20 A DC switching power supply
  • Custom mechanical linkage and base
  • Pivot joints, fasteners, and wiring
  • USB cable for ESP32 programming and testing

Technical Details & Wiring Connections

Motor 1 Connections:

  • ESP32 VIN (5 V) → DM542 PUL+
  • GPIO 18 → PUL−
  • ESP32 VIN (5 V) → DIR+
  • GPIO 19 → DIR−

Motor 2 Connections:

  • ESP32 VIN (5 V) → DM542 PUL+
  • GPIO 25 → PUL−
  • ESP32 VIN (5 V) → DIR+
  • GPIO 26 → DIR−

Note: ENA terminals were disconnected during the reported successful test setup. Driver and motor stages are powered via a 12 V, 20 A DC switching power supply.

Working Principle

  1. The ESP32 generates STEP pulses for each DM542 driver based on target coordinates.
  2. The DIR signal determines the rotation direction for each motor axis.
  3. Each DM542 driver controls and powers its corresponding NEMA 23 stepper motor.
  4. Motor rotation drives the interconnected mechanical linkage.
  5. Coordinated movement of both motors articulates the linkage according to the supplied coordinates.

Current Testing Status

  • The physical linkage prototype and base structure have been fabricated.
  • ESP32 STEP/DIR connections were integrated with both DM542 drivers.
  • The mechanism was tested with both motors working together in coordination.
  • The linkage moved according to the coordinates provided to the control system.

Technologies & Tools

ESP32, Embedded C/C++, DM542 Stepper Drivers, NEMA 23 Stepper Motors, STEP/DIR Control, Mechanical Linkage Design, Coordinated Kinematics, Prototyping, Electrical Wiring, and Troubleshooting.

Project Overview

The Autonomous Toilet Seat Mapping & Robotic Cleaning System is a custom-built robotic arm designed to systematically map the surface of a toilet seat and then use a motor-driven rotating brush to clean it. The arm is constructed from 3D-printed structural parts and is controlled via an ESP32-WROOM-32 microcontroller interfaced with a PCA9685 16-channel PWM driver. Five servo motors provide articulated motion across the base, shoulder, elbow, and wrist joints, while a DC geared motor drives the rotating cleaning brush.

Project Objectives

  • Design and fabricate a multi-joint robotic arm capable of reaching across a toilet seat surface.
  • Integrate a PCA9685 PWM driver with an ESP32 to control multiple servo channels simultaneously.
  • Drive a rotating cleaning brush using a DC geared motor and a dedicated motor driver.
  • Develop a motion sequence to map the toilet seat surface and execute a cleaning pass.
  • Produce all structural arm parts using 3D printing for rapid prototyping and iteration.

Hardware Components

  • ESP32-WROOM-32 development board
  • PCA9685 16-channel, 12-bit PWM servo driver (I²C)
  • Five servo motors (base, shoulder master, shoulder slave, elbow, wrist)
  • DC geared motor for rotating the cleaning brush
  • DC motor driver module for brush motor control
  • 3D-printed base, arm links, wrist mount, and servo mounting parts
  • Rotary cleaning brush attachment

Mechanical Design

Arm Link Dimensions:

  • Base to Shoulder: 20 cm
  • Shoulder to Elbow: 14 cm
  • Elbow to Wrist: 20 cm

Servo Channel Mapping

Joint PCA9685 Channel Initial Position
Base Channel 0 90°
Shoulder Master Channel 1 90°
Shoulder Slave Channel 2 90° (mirrors master)
Elbow Channel 3 90°
Wrist Channel 4 180°

Shoulder Master‑Slave Relationship

Shoulder Slave Angle = 180° − Shoulder Master Angle

The shoulder joint uses two opposing servo motors to provide balanced torque. The slave servo always moves to the supplementary angle of the master servo, ensuring both servos work in the same rotational direction on the joint without opposing each other.

Working Principle

  1. The ESP32 communicates with the PCA9685 over I²C to generate PWM signals for each servo channel.
  2. Servo positions are set to move the arm through a predefined mapping trajectory over the toilet seat surface.
  3. The shoulder master and slave servos actuate together (slave mirrors master) to lift and lower the arm with balanced force.
  4. Once positioned, the DC geared motor spins the rotating brush via the motor driver to perform cleaning.
  5. The arm traverses the surface in a planned pattern designed to cover the target area.

Technologies Used

ESP32-WROOM-32, PCA9685 PWM Driver, I²C Protocol, Servo Control, DC Geared Motor, DC Motor Driver, 3D Printing, Embedded C/C++, Robotics Arm Kinematics, Mechanical Prototyping.

Development Status & Future Improvements

  • 3D-printed structural parts have been fabricated and assembled.
  • ESP32 and PCA9685 integration is in active development for multi-servo coordination.
  • Initial arm motion sequences are being defined and tested.
  • Future improvements include closed-loop surface detection, sensor-guided path adjustment, and optimised cleaning coverage patterns.

Project Overview

The ESP32-Based Underwater ROV is a prototype underwater vehicle developed to explore custom EDF (Electric Ducted Fan) propulsion in a submerged environment. The vehicle uses an ESP32 microcontroller, two custom-designed and 3D-printed EDF propulsion units each driven by a BLDC motor, and two servo motors for directional control. Control is handled via a wired joystick using a reused controller. Motor operation, thrust generation in water, and directional movement have been tested. Electronics waterproofing and full underwater validation remain as future development work.

Key Features

  • ESP32 microcontroller for vehicle control logic
  • Two custom 3D-printed EDF propulsion units with BLDC motors
  • Two servo motors for directional control
  • Wired joystick control using a reused controller
  • Thrust generation and directional movement tested in water

Development Status & Future Work

  • Motor operation, thrust output, and directional movement have been successfully tested.
  • Electronics waterproofing is not yet complete.
  • Full sealed underwater validation remains as a future development phase.

Technologies Used

ESP32, BLDC Motors, EDF Propulsion Design, 3D Printing, Servo Control, Wired Joystick Interface, Embedded C/C++, Mechanical Prototyping.

Project Overview

The Autonomous Multi-Purpose UAV is a versatile quadcopter engineering platform developed to study autonomous flight, flight-controller tuning, GPS waypoint navigation, and multi-mission capabilities. The project integrates two distinct flight-controller configurations: a Pixhawk 2.4.8 system running ArduPilot with Mission Planner, and a RadioLink CrossFlight setup with dedicated radio-control and telemetry. The platform explores real-world applications in agricultural mapping, surveillance, and smart lightweight delivery.

Objectives

  • Design, assemble, and configure an autonomous multi-rotor aerial platform.
  • Implement and evaluate two flight-controller architectures: Pixhawk 2.4.8 (ArduPilot) and RadioLink CrossFlight.
  • Calibrate BLDC motors, electronic speed controllers (ESCs), and power distribution systems.
  • Achieve reliable manual flight, stable hovering (Loiter / AltHold), and autonomous waypoint missions.
  • Integrate high-precision GPS navigation, wireless telemetry, and automated landing sequences.
  • Explore aerial survey, agricultural field mapping, and smart delivery waypoint routing.

Flight Controller Configurations

Configuration 1: Pixhawk 2.4.8 (ArduPilot & Mission Planner)

  • 32-bit advanced autopilot running open-source ArduPilot (ArduCopter) firmware.
  • Integrated with Mission Planner Ground Control Station (GCS) for pre-flight calibration and waypoint mission programming.
  • External high-precision GPS/Compass module on mast for accurate geomagnetic and satellite positioning.
  • Bidirectional wireless telemetry radio for real-time inflight telemetry, battery voltage, and status monitoring.
  • Flight modes verified: Stabilize, Altitude Hold, Loiter (GPS hold), Auto (waypoint mission), and Return-To-Launch (RTL).

Configuration 2: RadioLink CrossFlight

  • Compact 32-bit flight controller with integrated vibration damping and high-sensitivity IMU sensors.
  • Dedicated RadioLink radio-control receiver and telemetry module for responsive pilot feedback.
  • Tuned for agile manual flight dynamics, responsive control loops, and GPS position stability.

Hardware Components & Technologies

  • Pixhawk 2.4.8 Autopilot / RadioLink CrossFlight Controller
  • High-thrust Brushless DC (BLDC) motors with matched propellers
  • 30 A Electronic Speed Controllers (ESCs)
  • High-precision GPS & Compass module mounted on an elevation mast
  • 2.4 GHz Multi-channel Radio Transmitter & Receiver
  • 433 MHz / 915 MHz Long-range Wireless Telemetry Radios
  • High-discharge LiPo battery pack with dedicated Power Distribution Board (PDB) & Current Sensor
  • Reinforced quadcopter airframe with anti-vibration mounting

Flight Capabilities & Verified Testing

  • Manual Flight Operations: Successfully tested responsive flight control across Stabilize and Altitude Hold modes.
  • GPS Navigation & Loiter: Verified stable position hold and drift rejection using multi-satellite GPS lock.
  • Autonomous Waypoint Missions: Pre-programmed autonomous route navigation executed successfully via Mission Planner.
  • Auto-Landing & Failsafes: Automated descent, landing sequence, and motor disarm verified on physical flight tests.
  • Wireless Telemetry: Live stream of GPS coordinates, altitude, battery health, and flight attitude to GCS laptop.

Applications & Development Scope

The platform is designed to support diverse aerial applications:

  • Agricultural Field Mapping: Aerial grid mapping missions to capture field geometry and terrain data. (Note: liquid chemical spraying mechanisms represent future phase development).
  • Perimeter Surveillance: Autonomous aerial patrolling, waypoint inspection, and remote reconnaissance.
  • Smart Delivery Routing: Exploration of GPS coordinate delivery corridors for emergency supplies or lightweight payloads. (Note: automated payload release dispensers are under development for future phases).
